## Shape

An Egyptian-temple pastiche in terracotta red. Strictly symmetric front:

- **Two pylon blocks** left and right, ~31% of the width each, plain red walls, slightly battered corners.
- **A recessed portico** between them (~37% of the width) with **four heavy columns** painted in bright bands.
- Everything stands on a **grey granite plinth** (~12% of the height) with small basement windows; a wide
  central staircase leads up to the door, with side stairs.
- A small raised **attic block** with a dark red roof sits above the middle.

Front width : height (to the pylon cornice) ≈ 1.9 : 1.

## Painted details (this is what makes it recognizable)

1. **Yellow frieze band with figures** across the top of both pylons (hunters, deer, birds) — the widest, brightest element.
2. Above the frieze a **comb of palmettes** and a flat cornice slab.
3. **Winged sun disk** over the portico: a red disk, two cobras, wide white-and-red wings.
4. **Columns** ringed with blue, red, green and white bands, with lotus capitals; the portico wall behind them is darker red.
5. Tall windows with light frames and grilles on the pylons; narrow tapered pilasters flank the portico.

## Around it

Globe street lamps (three globes per post), fir trees, flower beds, poster stands along the pavement.
The building faces the main street; the embankment and the river are behind it.

## Primitive plan

Boxes for the pylons, plinth and portico; columns as thin boxes or cylinders with painted rings as stacked
thin instanced boxes; the winged disk as a disk plus tapering wing slabs; frieze figures as a repeating row of
small instanced blocks (readable as a pattern, not as literal drawings); steps as stacked boxes.
